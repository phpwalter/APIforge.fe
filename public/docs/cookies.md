## 1. Purpose of This Page

This page explains how APIForge uses cookies and similar technologies, how you can manage your cookie preferences, and what choices are available to you when using the APIForge website, application, documentation, community areas, and related online services.

APIForge is a SaaS platform for creating, editing, validating, governing, and exporting OpenAPI documents. Because APIForge is both a web application and an API-design workspace, cookies may support account access, security, product preferences, usage analytics, and optional integrations.

This page should be read together with the APIForge Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Security Policy, Community Guidelines, and Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information page.

---

## 2. What Are Cookies?

Cookies are small text files stored on your browser or device when you visit a website or use a web application. Cookies help websites remember information about your session, preferences, authentication state, and interactions.

APIForge may also use similar technologies, including:

- Local storage
- Session storage
- Browser-based identifiers
- Device identifiers
- Pixels
- Tags
- Software development kits
- Log-based identifiers
- Analytics events
- Security tokens

For simplicity, this page refers to these technologies collectively as “cookies,” unless a more specific term is required.

---

## 3. How APIForge Uses Cookies

APIForge uses cookies to operate the product, protect user accounts, remember user preferences, improve performance, understand usage patterns, and support optional marketing or communication functions.

Cookies may be used for the following purposes:

1. Keeping you signed in
2. Maintaining secure sessions
3. Preventing unauthorized access
4. Remembering application preferences
5. Storing editor state and interface choices
6. Supporting workspace navigation
7. Measuring product usage and reliability
8. Detecting suspicious or abusive activity
9. Supporting customer support troubleshooting
10. Measuring the effectiveness of product pages or campaigns
11. Supporting optional third-party integrations

APIForge does not require optional advertising or marketing cookies for core product functionality.

## 4. Cookie Categories

APIForge groups cookies into the following categories.

---

## 4.1 Strictly Necessary Cookies

Strictly necessary cookies are required for APIForge to function. These cookies support core platform behavior and cannot be disabled through the APIForge cookie preference center.

These cookies may be used to:

- Authenticate users
- Maintain secure login sessions
- Protect against cross-site request forgery
- Detect suspicious activity
- Load the application securely
- Route requests correctly
- Remember privacy choices
- Support billing or subscription access
- Preserve application availability and reliability

Examples may include cookies or tokens related to:

- Session management
- Authentication state
- Security validation
- Load balancing
- Consent preference storage
- Account access
- Abuse prevention

Because these cookies are necessary to provide the service, disabling them in your browser may cause APIForge to stop working correctly.

---

## 4.2 Functional Cookies

Functional cookies help APIForge remember product settings and user preferences.

These cookies may support:

- Theme preferences
- Editor layout preferences
- Sidebar state
- Selected workspace or project
- Recently used views
- Preferred export settings
- Documentation display preferences
- Language or regional settings
- Accessibility preferences

For example, APIForge may remember whether you prefer a compact editor layout, a specific OpenAPI view, a preferred resource grouping mode, or a previously selected navigation panel.

Some functional preferences may also be stored within your APIForge account or within OpenAPI vendor extension metadata, such as `x-apiforge`, when those preferences need to travel with an API design document.

Functional cookies are intended to improve the user experience, but they are not required for APIForge’s core security or account access features.

---

## 4.3 Analytics Cookies

Analytics cookies help APIForge understand how users interact with the product and website. This information is used to improve reliability, usability, performance, onboarding, documentation, and product design.

Analytics cookies may help answer questions such as:

- Which product areas are used most often?
- Where do users encounter errors?
- Which pages are slow or unreliable?
- Which documentation pages are most helpful?
- Which onboarding steps cause confusion?
- Which browser or device environments need better support?

Analytics information may include:

- Page views
- Product events
- Session duration
- Referring pages
- Browser type
- Device type
- Approximate region
- Error events
- Performance metrics
- Feature usage patterns

APIForge should avoid using analytics data to inspect the private contents of your OpenAPI files, schemas, examples, security definitions, or internal API documentation unless specifically required to provide support, security, debugging, or another service you requested.

---

## 4.4 Performance Cookies

Performance cookies help APIForge measure and improve system speed, stability, and responsiveness.

These cookies or related telemetry events may help APIForge understand:

- Application load times
- API response times
- Editor rendering performance
- Export processing time
- Import processing time
- Validation latency
- Frontend error rates
- Backend service health

Performance cookies are used to improve the quality of the service and diagnose operational issues.

Security cookies help protect APIForge, customer workspaces, user accounts, OpenAPI documents, and related service infrastructure.

Security cookies may support:

- Session validation

- Brute-force protection

- Suspicious access monitoring
- Device recognition

- Incident investigation

Marketing cookies may support:

## 5. Cookies and APIForge Workspaces

APIForge may use cookies to support workspace behavior, including:

- Remembering your active workspace
- Remembering your active project
- Remembering recently opened OpenAPI documents
- Preserving temporary editor state
- Remembering collapsed panels or selected tabs
- Supporting import and export workflows
- Maintaining session continuity during editing

Cookies should not be treated as the authoritative source of an OpenAPI document. The authoritative API design content should be stored in APIForge’s backend systems, user-controlled exports, connected repositories, or the OpenAPI file itself.

---

## 6. Cookies and `x-apiforge` Metadata

APIForge may use OpenAPI Specification Extensions, such as `x-apiforge`, to preserve editor state, user preferences, workflow metadata, layout choices, notes, and other APIForge-specific information inside an OpenAPI document.

Cookie preferences and `x-apiforge` metadata are related but separate concepts.

Cookies may remember browser or session-level choices, such as:

- Whether a sidebar is collapsed
- Whether a preference banner has been dismissed
- Which theme was selected
- Which workspace was last opened

`x-apiforge` metadata may preserve document-level APIForge information, such as:

- Operation editor layout
- API design workflow state
- Internal APIForge notes
- Governance settings
- Display ordering
- Resource grouping preferences

APIForge should not use cookies as the primary storage mechanism for long-term OpenAPI document metadata.

## 8. How to Manage Cookies in Your Browser

You can also manage cookies through your browser settings. Most browsers allow you to:

- View cookies
- Delete cookies
- Block all cookies
- Block third-party cookies
- Clear cookies when the browser closes
- Set exceptions for specific websites
- Manage site permissions

Browser controls vary by browser and device. You should review the support materials for your browser to understand the available settings.

Blocking or deleting cookies may affect APIForge functionality. For example, you may be signed out, lose temporary preferences, or need to reconfigure certain settings.

## 9. Global Privacy Control and Similar Signals

Where required by applicable law, APIForge may recognize browser-based opt-out signals such as Global Privacy Control or similar technologies.

If APIForge recognizes such a signal, it may be treated as a request to opt out of certain types of sharing, targeted advertising, or optional tracking, depending on applicable law and APIForge’s technical implementation.

APIForge should continue to use strictly necessary cookies even when such signals are enabled, because those cookies are required to provide and secure the service.

Where APIForge recognizes legally required opt-out signals, those controls should be described in this page, the Privacy Policy, and the Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information page.

## 15. Cookie Retention

Cookies may remain on your device for different periods depending on their purpose.

Cookies may be:

- Session cookies, which expire when you close your browser
- Persistent cookies, which remain until they expire or are deleted
- Short-lived security cookies
- Longer-lived preference cookies
- Consent-management cookies used to remember privacy choices

APIForge should retain cookies only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purposes described in this page, unless a longer period is required or permitted by law.
